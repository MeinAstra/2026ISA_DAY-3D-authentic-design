# ISA Day 2026 — Student-Led Design & Digital Fabrication

*An independent design and engineering initiative by Jocelyn Xu, Secretary-General of the Student Council, ISA Wuhan.*

## The Story Behind the Project

This project began with a rather practical problem. While preparing for ISA Day 2026 as the Secretary-General of the Student Council, I learned that urgent procurement was not available under the school's existing arrangements. Rather than relying entirely on externally purchased merchandise, I began to wonder whether we could design and manufacture something ourselves.

Our school already had 3D printers, technical resources, and a community of students interested in science, engineering, and design. I saw an opportunity to bring these resources together and create something that was not only meaningful to our school community, but also representative of what students themselves could accomplish.

What started as a small experiment gradually developed into a collection of independently designed, 3D-printable school souvenirs.

## The Design Philosophy

I wanted these products to be more than ordinary merchandise with a school logo placed on top. I wanted each design to carry something distinctive about our school, while also reflecting my own interests in geometry, engineering, and aesthetics.

The first design features the ISA emblem and the words “ISA Wuhan School” on its front, with an engraved maple branch on the reverse, inspired by Fenglin Road (枫林路), where our school is located. The intention was to preserve a small connection to the physical place in which we spend so much of our student lives.

Another design, the ISA Orbital Keychain, takes a more experimental approach. It integrates the original geometric ISA emblem with a three-dimensional orbital structure, an elongated four-point star, and open negative spaces.

Rather than relying solely on color to distinguish different elements, I wanted to explore how geometry, light, depth, and spatial relationships could communicate a visual identity. The different structural heights allow the design to retain its character even when manufactured using a single-color or transparent filament.

This also reflects something I particularly enjoy about engineering: finding ways in which aesthetic intentions and manufacturing constraints can complement each other rather than compete.

## From Design to Manufacturing

The project involves much more than producing a digital model. Throughout its development, I have been exploring geometric reconstruction, CAD modeling, additive manufacturing, structural connectivity, material selection, slicing, and design for manufacturability.

With technical guidance from Mr. Howard Hong, I also began considering how these designs could move beyond individual prototypes and become small-batch manufactured products.

Our school's two AMS-compatible 3D printers have limited production capacity. Given the approaching ISA Day and the uncertainty of producing a sufficient number of items on campus, we decided to explore external 3D printing farms for batch manufacturing.

This introduced another set of practical considerations: production costs, delivery schedules, material availability, quality control, pricing, and anticipated demand. A preorder system was also proposed to reduce the risk of unnecessary production while allowing more members of the school community to participate.

The transition from a digital idea to a physical product has been an interesting learning experience in itself. A design is not necessarily successful simply because it looks good on a screen; it must also survive the realities of manufacturing and everyday use.

## My Vision as Secretary-General

As Secretary-General, I hope that the Student Council can be more than an organization that coordinates events and manages administrative tasks.

I would like it to become a platform through which students can turn their ideas into meaningful contributions to the school community.

For this project, I proposed that a portion of the proceeds, after covering manufacturing, transportation, and other necessary expenses, be reinvested into the school's STEM Center through the purchase of 3D printing filament.

The idea is to establish a small but sustainable cycle:

**Student creativity → Design and engineering → Physical products → Community participation → Reinvestment in student innovation**

Instead of treating school merchandise purely as something to purchase and sell, I hope we can use it as an opportunity to support student-led creation.

If the materials purchased through this initiative eventually help another student transform a sketch into a prototype, explore a new engineering idea, or simply experience the satisfaction of making something with their own hands, then this project will have accomplished something beyond its original purpose.

I also hope that future Student Council initiatives will provide more opportunities for students to contribute through their individual interests and abilities, whether those lie in engineering, art, science, entrepreneurship, or other creative disciplines.

## Project Status

This project is currently undergoing design refinement, prototype evaluation, and manufacturing preparation for ISA Day 2026.

The repository serves as a record of the design process, technical experiments, and the development of this initiative. Final production quantities, manufacturing costs, and the amount reinvested into the STEM Center will depend on actual production and sales outcomes.
